Berrak is a private web browser based on Firefox Focus. It blocks trackers, keeps nothing once you tap erase, and adds one thing Focus cannot do: it opens httpx:// pages, the HTTP over XMPP scheme of XEP-0332.

An httpx:// address names an XMPP account instead of a web server, for example httpx://web@example.org/. Berrak fetches the page over your own XMPP account, so a site can be served from a home network or a phone without a public web server, open ports or a domain of its own.

What Berrak does
• Opens httpx:// addresses typed in the address bar and followed from links.
• Follows links, submits forms, saves downloads, and goes back and forward on httpx pages.
• Shows httpx pages in a sanitised view: scripts on them never run.
• Keeps everything Firefox Focus does: tracker blocking, the erase button, no history.
• Optionally browses the ordinary web through an n146 exit (Settings, Advanced). The exit fetches each page itself, HTTPS included, so it sees everything on those pages and everything you type into them. Use only an exit you trust with that.

Privacy
Berrak sends nothing to its developer. There is no telemetry upload and no crash reporting service. Your XMPP account details stay in the app's own storage on your device. httpx traffic goes only to the XMPP server you configure.

Berrak is an independent fork of Mozilla's Firefox Focus. It is not produced or endorsed by Mozilla.
